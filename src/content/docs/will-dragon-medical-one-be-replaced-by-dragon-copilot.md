## Why this question is being asked

Dragon Copilot's launch messaging describes it as unifying the capabilities of Dragon Medical One
and DAX Copilot. For an organisation with hundreds of Dragon Medical One seats and a renewal
approaching, that reads like a countdown.

It is worth separating three different things that get run together:

- **Unification** — Dragon Copilot contains dictation capability derived from Dragon Medical One.
  This is published and clear.
- **Migration** — clinicians moving from one product to the other. This happens, and is a
  commercial and workflow decision.
- **Retirement** — Microsoft ceasing to sell or support Dragon Medical One. **No announcement of
  this was located.**

The first is documented. The second is a choice. The third is an assumption.

## What is actually published

Microsoft continues to maintain Dragon Medical One documentation, and runs a Dragon Medical One
learning series directed specifically at Australia and New Zealand covering fundamentals, voice
training, templates and step-by-step EMR commands. That is not the behaviour of a product being
wound down quietly, though it is also not a commitment.

<aside class="callout callout--warn">
<p><strong>What this page will not do.</strong> It will not give you a retirement date, because no

</aside>

## The practical position for existing sites

Renewal conversations are where the useful information lives. Roadmap detail, migration terms and
transition arrangements are routinely discussed with customers well before anything is published.
An organisation with a material number of seats has more leverage in that conversation than the
public documentation suggests.

## The short version

Dragon Copilot is Microsoft's AI assistant for clinicians. It brings together three things that used to
be separate products or separate jobs: dictation, ambient documentation, and the administrative work that
follows a consultation.

## What happens in the room

With the patient's consent, Dragon Copilot listens to the consultation. Not to transcribe it verbatim —
to understand it. At the end of the conversation, a structured clinical note is drafted from what was
discussed: history, examination, assessment, plan, in the format the clinician uses.

The clinician reads it, corrects anything that needs correcting, and signs. Nothing enters the record
unreviewed. Microsoft is unambiguous on this point: the AI drafts, the clinician validates, and the
clinician is accountable for what is signed.

## What it does beyond the note

**Dictation, unchanged.** Everything Dragon Medical One does — dictation into the record, custom
vocabulary, auto-texts, step-by-step voice commands — is in Dragon Copilot. This is an addition, not a
replacement.

**Clinical questions.** Ask a question during the consultation and get an answer drawn from trusted
medical sources, without leaving the record.

**The paperwork after.** Referral letters, orders and summaries drafted from the same conversation that
produced the note, rather than written from scratch afterwards.

## Where it works

Microsoft publishes the deepest integration detail for Epic, including an embedded window that opens
inside the encounter and a mechanism that places drafted sections directly into the Epic note template.

More broadly — and this is the part most coverage gets wrong — Microsoft does not certify clinical
systems. It publishes a list of supported *text controls*. A system built on a supported control gets
text directly in the field; anything else falls back to a dictation window you copy from. Which is why
the useful question is not "is my system supported" but "which control does my system use", and why
[which clinical systems Dragon works with](/emr/) is worth checking for your specific setup.

## How it is licensed

Dragon Copilot is licensed per clinician on a subscription basis, with different tiers reflecting how
much of the product a given clinician uses. Microsoft positions one option specifically for health
systems transitioning across from Dragon Medical One.

Because licensing terms, tiers and availability change and are not all published, the sensible step is to
get the current position for your organisation rather than working from a page. Ask us and we will confirm
it.

## Australian availability

Dragon Copilot is coming to the Australian market. Microsoft has not announced an Australian release
date.

In the meantime, [Dragon Medical One in Australia](/dragon-medical-one/) is available now, runs on
Australian infrastructure, and Microsoft publishes a migration path that carries saved commands,
vocabularies, templates and auto-texts across to Dragon Copilot. Organisations that start on Dragon
Medical One now are building assets that transfer, not work they will repeat.

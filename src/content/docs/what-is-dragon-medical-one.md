## What it does

Dragon Medical One is clinical speech recognition. A doctor speaks, and the words appear in the patient
record — in the consultation note, the referral letter, the discharge summary, the operation report.

The practical difference from general dictation software is the vocabulary. Dragon Medical One ships
knowing clinical language: drug names, anatomy, procedures, abbreviations and the phrasing clinicians
actually use, across more than 90 specialties and subspecialties. You are not training it to recognise
"metoprolol" or "cholecystectomy" — it already knows them.

Typical dictation runs at around 160 words a minute. Most clinicians type at a fraction of that.

## Why Australian clinicians use it

Documentation is the part of the job that follows people home. Dragon Medical One exists to close that
gap — finishing the note during or immediately after the consultation, rather than at nine o'clock that
night.

It is used across Australian hospitals, specialist rooms and general practice, and it has been available
here for years through authorised resellers.

## Australian infrastructure

This matters more than most product pages admit. Dragon Medical One runs on Australian regional
infrastructure, with a documented Australian endpoint and an Australian resource centre. Australian
clinical dictation is not being routed offshore.

For hospital and government buyers running a privacy impact assessment, that is a materially different
starting position from a product with no Australian region.

## Custom vocabulary, auto-texts and commands

Three features do most of the heavy lifting in real practice:

**Custom vocabulary** — add the words your practice uses that no general medical dictionary contains:
local hospital names, referring doctors, specific equipment, the phrasing of your own department.

**Auto-texts** — say a short phrase and insert a whole templated block. "Normal cardio exam" can expand
into a full paragraph. For anyone writing the same paragraph forty times a week, this is where the time
actually comes back.

**Step-by-step commands** — voice commands that drive the software itself, opening a form, moving between
fields, saving a record. This is what turns dictation into a workflow rather than just typing by voice.

All three carry across to Dragon Copilot when you migrate, which is the practical argument for building
them now rather than waiting.

## How it is licensed

Dragon Medical One is licensed as a subscription per clinician rather than a one-off purchase. Because
deployment usually touches the clinical system, the dictation hardware and the network path, most
organisations buy it with implementation support rather than as a download.

Hospitals and larger practices are typically deployed under a managed arrangement covering configuration,
Citrix or virtual desktop setup, microphone hardware and clinician onboarding.

## Where Dragon Copilot fits

Dragon Copilot is Microsoft's next step: everything Dragon Medical One does, plus ambient documentation —
where the software listens to the consultation with the patient's consent and drafts the note from the
conversation itself, rather than from dictation.

Dragon Copilot is coming to the Australian market. Microsoft has not announced an Australian release
date, and Dragon Medical One remains actively sold, documented and supported in the meantime, with no
retirement date announced.

Microsoft publishes a documented migration path between them that carries your saved commands,
vocabularies, templates and auto-texts across. Starting on Dragon Medical One now is not a dead end — it
is the on-ramp.
